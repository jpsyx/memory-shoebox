Memory Shoebox Prototype

An uncurated archive is a fridge door, not a gallery.

Four surfaces in one world. Nobody curates: everything goes up, so a burst of forty near-identical frames is one stack you fan out rather than forty tiles burying the rest of the day. Day and Messy are the chosen direction and load by default; the switches above stay only so the alternatives can still be compared.

The pile Three days, a fixed date spine, and one burst stacked. One frame Full frame, the burst it came from, and the notes on it. One video Notes pinned to a moment, standing on a measured bar. Arriving from a link What a stranger sees, which is nothing but the count. A new circle The same world with every photograph removed.
The idea being tested
Volume without curation. The uploader dumps a whole day and thinks about nothing; the software takes on the sorting. Stacks collapse bursts, the date spine never scrolls away, counts are the biggest type on the page, and the one accent colour means exactly one thing: you have not seen this yet.
Day, chosen
A pale blue panel with navy ink. Porcelain, Slate and Night remain switchable for comparison. Each panel is exactly four inks, with every other value computed as a mix of those four, and each accent is solved twice: once to stay readable on the panel, once on the white prints. On a dark panel those pull in opposite directions, which is why Night's two differ most.
Messy, chosen
Nobody straightens a fridge door. Every print gets a small rotation and offset so the wall reads as stuck up rather than laid out, and prints overlap their neighbours. The angles are seeded from each print's position, so they stay put across reloads instead of reshuffling. An opened burst stays tidy on purpose: that is where you are hunting for one frame, and forty crooked thumbnails is noise rather than character.
Where the arrangement lives
Tidy versus messy is an instance-level setting in the product: chosen once by whoever runs the deployment, the same for everyone in the circle, and not a per-viewer preference. It is a switch here only so the two can be compared. Recorded in PRODUCT.md; not built.
What is real and what is not
The photographs and the video are yours and sit in a gitignored folder. The pile is built from real frames of your video, which is why it looks like a real burst of nearly identical shots. The per-day totals (212, 168, 94) and the 2,147 archive total are written demonstration figures, larger than what is on screen, to show how the counts read at true scale. Every name and note is written demonstration content, not a real person's words.
What this round decides
The vibe only: palette, type, how a pile is held, how a burst collapses, how a note looks. Not features, not navigation, not the data model.
